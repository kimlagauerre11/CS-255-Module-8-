DriverPass Project

DriverPass was a client that wanted a system to help customers prepare for DMV driving tests. The system needed to include online classes, practice tests, training packages, and behind-the-wheel lessons. Customers also needed to be able to create accounts, manage appointments, and track their progress, while employees needed different levels of access depending on their role.

What I Did Well

I think I did well with taking the information from the client interview and turning it into actual system requirements. Once I understood what DriverPass was trying to accomplish, it became easier to break the system down into the different users, functions, and processes. I also got more comfortable using UML diagrams to show how different parts of the system work together.

What I Would Revise

If I could revise one part of my work, I would probably spend more time refining some of the UML diagrams. The biggest challenge was deciding how much detail to include without making them too crowded. I would focus on keeping the important information while making the flow and relationships easier to read.

Understanding User Needs

I started by looking at what DriverPass actually needed users to accomplish instead of going straight into the technical design. Customers needed access to classes and tests, but they also needed to schedule and change driving lessons. Employees had different responsibilities, so the system also needed different levels of access. Those needs became the requirements and eventually the UML diagrams.

Considering the user’s needs is important because a system can technically work and still fail if it does not solve the problem the customer actually has.

My Approach to Software Design

I usually approach software design by first figuring out what needs to happen, why it matters, and then how the system can support it. In the future, I would continue using requirements gathering, process modeling, UML diagrams, and feedback from users or stakeholders before getting too far into development.

I also think asking questions early is important. It is easier to clear up a misunderstanding during the design process than after the system has already been built.
